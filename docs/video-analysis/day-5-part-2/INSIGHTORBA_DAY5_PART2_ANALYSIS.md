Day 5 Part 2 is the afternoon/evening block of the same internship shift. It continues live chat work, but the trainer's main emphasis shifts to conversion quality:

## Regulations reconstructed

### 1. Intrigue is the main conversion lever

Trainer's diagnosis: when the trainee writes normally but clients do not click/book, the problem is often the intrigue.

Rules:
- the intrigue must hook the client's emotion;
- weak or generic hooks should be replaced;
- different experts may convert with different themes;
- if a theme fails repeatedly, stop forcing it and try another;
- saved documents with intrigue ideas should be reviewed and reused as inspiration;
- each intrigue needs a matching "what will be revealed in session" answer.

Product implication: the composer should support a library of intrigue themes tied to conversation stage and client topic, but should also flag copy-paste risk.

### 2. Templates are examples, not final text

Trainer repeatedly corrects word-for-word copying:
- use examples as structure;
- rewrite in your own words;
- change wording across clients;
- do not paste identical messages into many chats;
- keep the expert's own voice.

Product implication: an assistant layer can suggest variants and warn about repeated identical outbound text.

### 3. Coupon rules continue

Day 5 Part 2 reinforces coupon behavior:
- check existing discount state;
- match promo to platform/source such as LV/PB/IB;
- send promo today if it expires today;
- do not overcomplicate if client reads it tomorrow;
- send promo/instructions as a separate normal message after Book Now;
- if code does not work for platform/account reasons, route to support.

Product implication: coupon should be a structured object with expiration, platform, already-used status, and send-after-offer instruction.

### 4. Old Book Now vs fresh flow

If a client responded soon after a Book Now, continue objection handling. If too much time has passed, the expert may need to re-open with a fresh free reading / reactivation approach.

Product implication: `lastBookNowAt` and `postBookNowModeExpiresAt` matter. Objection mode should not be permanent forever.

### 5. Paid-session silence is high risk

Trainer gives a strong warning around session gaps:
- do not leave client without messages for several minutes;
- around 3 minutes is treated as a serious gap;
- client may go to support or request refund;
- operator may lose credit/money;
- if there was a delay, send additional transition/support messages, but do not rely on after-session compensation.

Product implication: paid-session mode needs a countdown, outbound cadence monitor, warning at idle thresholds, and refund-risk signal.

### 6. Hidden/deleted/banned chat states

Trainer discusses states where:
- user is banned;
- chat was deleted/hidden from client;
- support/admin can still see history;
- expert may not see all current history after refresh;
- client may not see previous correspondence anymore;
- work/time may still count.

Product implication: the conversation contract needs explicit `visibilityState` and `supportVisibility` fields.

### 7. Work-time is still message based

Trainer confirms an important metric rule:
- Book Now button itself does not count as time;
- sent messages count toward work;
- dashboard/statistics are used to evaluate hours and efficiency;
- around 7:48 worked time is praised;
- around 10-12% efficiency is good;
- 8% may still be okay;
- very low efficiency needs context and chat-quality review.

Product implication: performance metrics must distinguish button clicks, drafts, and actual outbound text messages.

### 8. Expert should focus, not scatter

Trainer warns against jumping across too many chats:
- if you take a chat, work it properly;
- jumping everywhere makes the process harder;
- finish current work before moving on where possible;
- use `Unanswered`/field cleanup before ending.

### 9. Tomorrow preparation: "поднятия"

At the end, trainer tells trainees:
- read sent files/materials before tomorrow;
- review trainer's successful example chats;
- prepare rewritten templates;
- "поднятия" will be a major next-day topic;
- examples include a regular/paying client where reactivation produced many bookings.

Product implication: the system should support a playbook/training-materials area linked to conversation type, such as reactivation/lift.

## Main takeaway

Day 5 Part 2 shows that the next competitive layer is conversion craft and supervisor control. The cabinet must help the expert not only "answer" but choose a working intrigue, avoid stale/copy-paste hooks, keep paid sessions active, handle hidden/support states, and track metrics correctly.

The strongest Confideline abstraction from this part is:

`intrigue.theme + postBookNow age + coupon state + paid-session cadence + visibility/support state + performance telemetry`.

That gives the workspace enough context to guide the operator and enough structure for mentor/admin review.
